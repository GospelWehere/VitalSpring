1.0 Introduction

Vital Spring Medical Center is a local outpatient medical facility that provides consultations and related healthcare services. The center currently manages patient records and appointments with paper folders, handwritten registers and a physical appointment book. Staff members register patients manually, search for folders before consultations, write appointment details into a book and communicate changes mainly through telephone calls.

This method becomes difficult to control as the number of patients increases. A missing folder can delay a consultation, an unclear schedule can result in two patients being assigned to the same doctor at the same time, and management cannot quickly obtain dependable information about attendance or doctor workload. The proposed Vital Spring Medical Center Clinic Appointment System will automate the principal activities involved in patient registration, doctor availability and appointment scheduling.

Purpose of this document: To define the project scope, identify the stakeholders and determine whether the proposed appointment system is technically, economically and operationally feasible, as required in Step 1 of the project assignment.

2.0 Project Charter

2.1 Project Title

Vital Spring Medical Center Clinic Appointment System

2.2 Problem Statement

Vital Spring Medical Center depends on paper folders and a physical appointment book for its patient and appointment activities. This manual system is inefficient and creates the following problems:

  1. Scheduling conflicts: Staff members may allocate the same doctor and time period to more than one patient because availability is checked manually.
  2. Difficulty retrieving patient records: Employees must search through shelves and files to find a returning patient's folder. Misfiled records may not be found when required.
  3. Lost or damaged patient history: Paper records can be misplaced, torn, exposed to moisture or handled by unauthorized persons. A damaged folder may contain information that cannot easily be reconstructed.
  4. Long waiting times: Patients wait while staff register information repeatedly, locate folders, confirm doctor availability and organize the consultation queue.
  5. Administrative burden: Receptionists and records personnel spend substantial time writing, copying, sorting and counting appointment information.
  6. Poor appointment communication: Confirmations, reminders, rescheduling information and cancellations depend on manual telephone calls. Patients may therefore forget appointments or arrive at the wrong time.
  7. Limited management information: Attendance figures, cancellations, missed appointments and doctor workload must be counted manually and may not be accurate.
  8. Weak privacy control: Physical folders do not provide reliable role-based access or an electronic record of who viewed or changed confidential patient information.

These problems reduce staff productivity, inconvenience patients and may affect the continuity and confidentiality of care. Vital Spring Medical Center therefore needs an automated system that centralizes appointment and patient information and makes it available only to authorized users.

2.3 Project Goals

The main goal of the project is to design an automated clinic appointment system that improves the speed, accuracy and security of appointment management at Vital Spring Medical Center.

The system is expected to achieve the following specific goals:

  1. Provide a centralized electronic profile for every registered patient.
  2. Allow patients or reception staff to book appointments without relying only on a physical appointment book.
  3. Allow appointments to be rescheduled or cancelled and keep their current status visible to authorized users.
  4. Allow doctors to define or update their available consultation periods.
  5. Check doctor availability before an appointment is accepted and prevent conflicting bookings.
  6. Give doctors quick access to their daily schedule and relevant patient history before consultation.
  7. Allow authorized clinical personnel to add consultation notes to a patient's medical history.
  8. Send appointment confirmations and reminders to patients through available communication channels.
  9. Reduce the time spent searching for records and manually preparing appointment information.
  10. Generate basic reports on clinic attendance, missed appointments and doctor utilization.
  11. Protect patient information through user authentication, role-based access, backups and accountability records.

3.0 Feasibility Analysis

The feasibility analysis determines whether the proposed Vital Spring Medical Center Clinic Appointment System can be developed and used successfully. In accordance with the assignment, the study considers technical, economic and operational feasibility.

3.1 Technical Feasibility

Technical feasibility examines whether the hardware, software and technical skills required for the system are available and reliable.

3.1.3 Availability and Reliability of the Technology

The proposed system can be built with established web and relational database technologies. Computers, networking equipment, backup devices, web hosting and communication services are available in Nigeria. The use of a browser-based system reduces the need to install separate application software on every workstation and allows the interface to work on different screen sizes.

The principal technical concerns are unreliable electricity, internet interruptions, equipment failure and unauthorized access. These concerns can be reduced with uninterruptible power supplies, backup electricity, a dependable hosting arrangement, secure network configuration, individual user accounts, encrypted connections, regular software updates and tested backups. Technical support must also be available to correct faults and manage the system after implementation.

Technical feasibility conclusion: The project is technically feasible because the required hardware and software are available and use reliable, established technology. Vital Spring Medical Center must budget for power protection, security, backups and continuing technical support.

3.2.3 Simple Cost-Benefit Analysis

Estimated initial cost: ₦7,300,000

Estimated annual operating cost: ₦1,400,000

Estimated annual benefit: ₦4,500,000

Estimated annual net benefit: ₦4,500,000 - ₦1,400,000 = ₦3,100,000

Simple payback period: ₦7,300,000 / ₦3,100,000 = approximately 2.35 years

The result means that, if the estimated benefits are achieved, the initial investment may be recovered in approximately two years and four months. The system would then continue to provide financial and non-financial benefits, subject to annual operating costs.

Economic feasibility conclusion: The project is economically feasible because the expected savings and improved use of appointment capacity can recover the estimated investment within a reasonable period. Management should confirm actual prices, patient volume and staff-time savings before approving expenditure.

3.3 Operational Feasibility

Operational feasibility considers whether clinic staff and patients will be able to use the proposed system effectively in their normal activities.

3.3.1 Ability of Staff to Use the System

The main system tasks, such as searching for a patient, selecting an available appointment time, confirming an arrival and viewing a daily schedule, are similar to activities already performed manually. A clear interface and role-based menus will make these activities easier to learn. Receptionists and records personnel will require the most detailed training because they create and update a large amount of information. Doctors and nurses will require focused training on schedules, patient history, clinical notes and secure use of the system.

Some employees may resist the new system because they are comfortable with the appointment book or fear making mistakes. They should be involved during requirements gathering and interface review. A pilot period should be used before full implementation so that problems can be corrected without interrupting the whole clinic.

3.3.3 Patient Data Privacy

Patient privacy is essential because the system will store personal and medical information. Vital Spring Medical Center must handle this information in accordance with the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 and professional duties of medical confidentiality. The following operational controls are required:

  • Every staff member must use an individual account instead of a shared username.
  • Users must only see the information required for their assigned duties.
  • Passwords must be protected, and users must sign out or lock the screen when leaving a workstation.
  • Important access and changes must be recorded so that inappropriate activity can be investigated.
  • Appointment messages must contain only the minimum information necessary and must not reveal detailed medical information.
  • Backups must be protected and tested so patient records can be restored after a failure.
  • Staff must receive confidentiality and data-protection training before using real patient records.
  • Incorrect records, suspected unauthorized access and lost devices must be reported immediately through an established procedure.

Patients who cannot or do not wish to book online should still be able to make appointments through the reception desk or telephone. This ensures that the system improves access rather than excluding patients with limited internet access or digital skills.

Operational feasibility conclusion: The system is operationally feasible. Clinic staff can use it effectively if the interface is simple, training is practical, management supports the change, patients retain an assisted booking option and privacy rules are enforced.

4.0 Conclusion

The manual appointment and patient-record process at Vital Spring Medical Center causes scheduling conflicts, slow record retrieval, lost information, long waiting times and excessive administrative work. The proposed Clinic Appointment System will centralize patient profiles, doctor availability and appointment scheduling while providing reminders, authorized access to history and basic management reports.

The study finds that the project is technically feasible because the necessary hardware and software are available and reliable. It is economically feasible because the simple cost-benefit analysis estimates an annual net benefit of ₦3,100,000 and a payback period of approximately 2.35 years. It is operationally feasible because its functions match existing clinic activities and can be learned through role-specific training. Patient privacy can be improved through individual accounts, restricted access, staff training, activity records and protected backups.

Vital Spring Medical Center should therefore proceed to Step 2, Requirements Analysis and Data Modeling, where user stories, functional requirements, non-functional requirements, a Context-Level Data Flow Diagram and a Level-0 Data Flow Diagram will be prepared.
